Topic 1

Pricing: Meaning & Definition

Price is the amount of money charged for a product or service. It is the sum of all values that customers exchange for the benefits of having or using the product. Price is the only element in the marketing mix that produces revenue; all other elements represent costs.

Definition of Price

According to Philip Kotler, "Price is the amount of money charged for a product or service. More broadly, price is the sum of all the values that consumers exchange for the benefits of having or using the product or service."

Real World Example
When you buy a cup of Starbucks coffee for ₹300, the price includes: the coffee beans, milk, cup, barista service, store ambiance, Wi-Fi access, brand experience, and status symbol. You are paying for the total value, not just the coffee.
Exam Tip
Remember: Price is the ONLY element of the 4Ps that generates revenue. Product, Place, and Promotion all generate costs.
References
  • Kotler, P., & Keller, K. L. — Marketing Management, 15th Edition
  • Nagle, T. — The Strategy and Tactics of Pricing
Topic 2

The Role of Pricing in Marketing

Pricing plays a critical role in determining a company's profitability, market positioning, and brand perception. It directly affects demand, revenue, and customer behavior.

Key Roles of Pricing

1. Revenue Generation

Price directly determines revenue. Even a small price change can significantly impact profitability. For example, a 1% price increase can increase operating profit by 8-11% in many industries.

2. Positioning and Brand Image

Price signals quality. Premium prices create perception of high quality (e.g., Apple, Mercedes). Low prices signal value and affordability (e.g., Dmart, Nirma).

3. Competitive Tool

Price is used to compete with rivals. Companies can engage in price wars (lowering prices to gain market share) or focus on non-price competition (differentiation).

4. Demand Management

Price affects demand. Higher prices reduce demand; lower prices increase demand. Companies use price to manage seasonal demand (surge pricing) or clear excess inventory (discounts).

5. Customer Value Communication

Price communicates the value proposition. Customers evaluate whether the product's benefits justify the price (value for money).

Topic 3

Importance of Pricing

Pricing is one of the most important strategic decisions a company makes. It directly affects profitability, customer perception, and long-term business survival.

Why Pricing is Important

Importance Explanation Example
Direct Impact on Profitability Price × Quantity = Revenue. A small price increase can significantly boost profits. Price is the only revenue-generating P. If a company with 10% profit margin increases price by 10%, profits can double (if demand remains stable).
Signals Quality Customers often assume higher price means higher quality. Price creates perception. Titan watches priced higher than generic watches — perceived as better quality.
Competitive Advantage Strategic pricing can win market share or create differentiation. Jio's low pricing disrupted Indian telecom market, gaining crores of customers.
Demand Management Price controls demand — higher prices reduce demand, lower prices increase demand. Surge pricing by Ola/Uber during peak hours manages limited supply.
Customer Acquisition & Retention Introductory low prices attract new customers. Loyalty discounts retain existing customers. Amazon Prime's annual subscription encourages customer loyalty and repeat purchases.
Breakeven and Survival Price must cover costs for business survival. Below cost pricing leads to losses and closure. During COVID, many small businesses priced at breakeven just to survive.
Key Insight

Pricing is not just about covering costs. It is a strategic tool that shapes brand perception, drives demand, and determines long-term profitability. Companies that treat pricing as an afterthought leave money on the table.

Factors Influencing Pricing Decisions

Pricing decisions are influenced by a combination of internal factors (within the company's control) and external factors (outside the company's control).

Internal Factors

Factor Description Example
Costs of Production Fixed costs (rent, salaries) + Variable costs (raw materials, labor). Price must cover costs for profitability. A company with high R&D costs (pharmaceuticals) must price higher to recover investment.
Marketing Objectives Survival (lowest prices), profit maximization, market share growth, product quality leadership. Jio priced low to gain market share. Apple prices high for quality leadership.
Brand Image Premium brands command higher prices. Value brands must keep prices low. Titan vs. Sonata — both from Tata Group, but different pricing.
Product Life Cycle Stage Introduction (high or low price), Growth (stable), Maturity (price competition), Decline (discounts). Phones launch at high price, drop over time.

External Factors

Factor Description Example

Customer Demand & Price Elasticity Elastic demand (price sensitive) requires lower prices. Inelastic demand (necessities) can handle higher prices.弹性需求（价格敏感）需要较低价格。非弹性需求（必需品）可以接受更高价格。 Petrol (inelastic) — price increase doesn't reduce demand much. Topic 5

Setting the Price: Six-Step Process

Setting the price involves a systematic six-step process from setting objectives to selecting the final price. This is based on Kotler's pricing framework.
1Select Pricing Objective
↓
2Determine Demand
↓
3Estimate Costs
↓
4Analyze Competitors' Pricing
↓
5Select Pricing Method
↓
6Select Final Price

Step 1: Select Pricing Objective

Companies must first decide what they want to achieve with pricing. Common objectives include:

  • Survival: Short-term objective during economic crisis or overcapacity — price just enough to cover costs.
  • Profit Maximization: Set price to maximize current profits (often used in introductory stage).
  • Market Share Growth: Set low price to gain market share (penetration pricing).
  • Product Quality Leadership: Set premium price to signal superior quality (skimming pricing).
  • Social Responsibility: Set prices that are fair and accessible (e.g., life-saving drugs).
Example — Jio's Pricing Objective
When Jio entered Indian telecom, its objective was market share growth. It offered free services for 6 months, then priced significantly lower than competitors. Within 3 years, Jio gained over 400 million customers and became market leader.

Step 2: Determine Demand

Understand how price affects demand. Key concepts:

  • Price Elasticity of Demand: How sensitive demand is to price changes.
    • Elastic Demand: Small price change causes large demand change (luxury goods, soft drinks). Price increases REDUCE revenue.
    • Inelastic Demand: Price change has little effect on demand (necessities, petrol, medicines). Price increases INCREASE revenue.
Price Elasticity = (% Change in Quantity Demanded) ÷ (% Change in Price)
Example — Elastic vs Inelastic Demand
Elastic (Soft drinks): If Coca-Cola increases price by 10%, demand might drop 20%. Total revenue decreases.
Inelastic (Petrol): If petrol price increases by 10%, demand might drop only 2%. Total revenue increases.

Step 3: Estimate Costs

Understanding costs ensures that price covers expenses and generates profit.

Cost Type Definition Example
Fixed Costs Costs that do not change with production volume Rent, salaries, insurance, machinery depreciation
Variable Costs Costs that change directly with production volume Raw materials, packaging, direct labor, electricity
Total Costs Fixed Costs + Variable Costs Sum of all costs
Breakeven Point Quantity where Total Revenue = Total Costs No profit, no loss — the survival point
Breakeven Point (units) = Fixed Costs ÷ (Price − Variable Cost per unit)
Example — Breakeven Calculation
A company sells pens at ₹10 each. Fixed costs = ₹1,00,000. Variable cost per pen = ₹6.
Contribution per pen = ₹10 − ₹6 = ₹4.
Breakeven point = ₹1,00,000 ÷ ₹4 = 25,000 pens.
The company must sell 25,000 pens to cover all costs. After 25,001 pens, every sale generates profit.

Step 4: Analyze Competitors' Pricing

Understand competitors' prices, costs, and reactions. Three positioning options:

  • Above Competition: Premium pricing (higher price, higher perceived value)
  • At Competition: Parity pricing (same price, compete on other factors)
  • Below Competition: Economy pricing (lower price, value positioning)
Example — Hotel Pricing in India
Taj Hotels: Premium pricing (above competition) — ₹15,000+/night
Ginger Hotels: Economy pricing (below competition) — ₹2,000-3,000/night
Radisson: At-competition pricing — ₹6,000-8,000/night

Step 5: Select Pricing Method

1. Cost-Based Pricing (Markup Pricing)

Formula: Price = Cost + (Cost × Markup Percentage)

Example: If cost = ₹100, markup = 50% → Price = ₹100 + ₹50 = ₹150

Pros: Simple, ensures profit. Cons: Ignores demand and competition.

2. Break-Even Pricing (Target Profit Pricing)

Formula: Price = Fixed Costs/Breakeven Quantity + Variable Cost

Example: Fixed costs ₹2,00,000, target quantity 10,000 units, variable cost ₹20 → Price = ₹2,00,000/10,000 + ₹20 = ₹20 + ₹20 = ₹40

3. Value-Based Pricing (Customer Perception)

Price based on perceived value to customer, not cost. Used by premium brands.

Example: Starbucks charges ₹300 for coffee that costs ₹50 to make. Customers pay for experience, ambiance, and brand.

4. Competition-Based Pricing (Going Rate)

Price based on competitors' prices — follow the market leader.

Example: Petrol prices are similar across all pumps (IOCL, BPCL, HPCL follow each other).

Step 6: Select Final Price

Consider psychological factors, company policies, and channel implications:

  • Psychological Pricing: ₹999 instead of ₹1000 (odd-even pricing)
  • Reference Price: "Was ₹2000, now ₹1500"
  • Channel Margins: Ensure distributors and retailers get adequate margins
  • Company Policies: Price consistency across regions, no predatory pricing
Example — Final Price Selection for iPhone
Step 1 Objective: Product quality leadership (premium positioning)
Step 2 Demand: Inelastic for loyal Apple customers
Step 3 Costs: High R&D + manufacturing + marketing
Step 4 Competitors: Samsung, OnePlus, Google Pixel
Step 5 Method: Value-based pricing (premium)
Step 6 Final: ₹79,900 (psychological pricing . . . → ends with 900)
Result: Premium price, premium brand perception, high margins.
Topic 6

Adapting the Price

Companies often need to adapt their prices for different situations, customer segments, geographies, and time periods. This section covers geographical pricing, discounts, promotional pricing, differentiated pricing, transfer pricing, and dynamic pricing.

1. Geographical Pricing

Adjusting prices based on customer location due to shipping costs, taxes, and local market conditions.

Type Description Example
FOB (Free on Board) Pricing Customer pays shipping from factory door Buyer pays actual courier charges
Uniform Delivered Pricing Same price to all locations regardless of distance Amazon offers same price across India (free shipping)
Zone Pricing Different prices for different geographic zones Petrol prices vary by state due to different taxes
Basing Point Pricing Price calculated from a specific base location Steel industry — all prices quoted from a basing point city

2. Price Discounts & Allowances

\{Reduction for buying in bulkReduction for buying off-season
Type Description Example
Cash Discount Reduction for prompt payment "2/10, n/30" — 2% discount if paid within 10 days, full amount in 30 days
Quantity Discount Buy 1 soap ₹30, Buy 3 soaps ₹80 (saving ₹10)
Seasonal Discount AC cheaper in winter, heaters cheaper in summer
Trade Discount Discount to channel partners (distributors, retailers) MRP ₹100, distributor gets 20% off, retailer gets 10% off
Promotional Allowance Payment to dealers for advertising or display Company gives retailer ₹5000 for new product display

3. Promotional Pricing

Temporary price reductions to stimulate demand, clear inventory, or attract customers.

  • Loss Leader Pricing: Selling a product below cost to attract customers (e.g., Dmart sells some items at very low prices to bring customers in).
  • Special Event Pricing: Discounts during festivals, holidays (e.g., Diwali sale, Black Friday, Amazon Great Indian Festival).
  • Cash Rebates: Refund after purchase (e.g., buy car, get ₹50,000 cashback).
  • Buy One Get One Free (BOGO): "Buy 1 Get 1 Free" or "Buy 2 Get 1 Free".
  • Low-Interest Financing: "0% EMI for 6 months" on electronics.
Example — Promotional Pricing in India
Amazon Great Indian Festival: Massive discounts during October each year — "80% off + bank offers + no cost EMI".
Zomato Pro: "Free delivery" promotions to attract new users.
Cred: Partner offers like "₹500 off on Swiggy" to drive app engagement.

4. Differentiated Pricing (Price Discrimination)

Charging different prices to different customer segments for the same product. This is legal as long as it's not based on discrimination protected by law (race, religion, gender).

Different prices for different customer groups
Type Description Example
Customer Segment Pricing Student discounts, senior citizen discounts, early bird pricing
Product Form Pricing Different versions of product at different prices iPhone 15: ₹79,900; iPhone 15 Pro: ₹1,34,900 (same software, different hardware)
Location Pricing Same product, different price by location Cinema ticket: ₹200 in small city, ₹800 in metro
Time Pricing Different prices by time of day, day of week, season Uber surge pricing (peak hours cost more), off-peak flight discounts
Example — Differentiated Pricing in India
Railway Tickets: Senior citizens get 40-50% discount. Students get 25-50% discount.
Movie Tickets: Weekday shows cheaper than weekends. Morning shows cheaper than evening shows.
Oyo Rooms: Same room priced differently on app vs walk-in vs aggregator.

5. Transfer Pricing

Transfer pricing is the price charged when one division or subsidiary of a company sells products or services to another division or subsidiary within the same company. It is an internal price, not visible to external customers.

Transfer Pricing affects profit allocation between different divisions, tax liabilities across countries, and performance evaluation of business units.

Methods of Transfer Pricing

  • Market-Based Transfer Price: Using external market price as internal price.
  • Cost-Based Transfer Price: Using cost of production (full cost or variable cost).
  • Negotiated Transfer Price: Divisions negotiate the internal price.
Example — Transfer Pricing (Indian Context)
Tata Motors: The division that makes engines sells them to the division that assembles cars. The price charged internally is transfer pricing. This affects how profit is allocated between divisions.
Infosys: Indian parent company might charge its US subsidiary for services. This transfer price affects taxes paid in India vs USA.
ITC: Tobacco division sells to packaging division internally — transfer pricing determines cost allocation.
Important — Tax Compliance

Transfer pricing is heavily regulated to prevent profit shifting to low-tax countries. In India, the Income Tax Act (Section 92-92F) requires arm's length pricing — internal prices should match what independent companies would charge. Non-compliance leads to penalties.

6. Dynamic Pricing (Surge Pricing & Auction Pricing)

Dynamic Pricing is the practice of varying prices in real-time based on demand, supply, time, customer behavior, and other factors.

A. Surge Pricing

Prices increase when demand exceeds supply. Used by ride-sharing, delivery apps, hotels, and events.

Example — Surge Pricing in India
Uber/Ola: During heavy rain, office closing time, or New Year's Eve, prices can increase 2-3x. The app shows "Surge pricing is active" and customers decide whether to accept.
Swiggy/Zomato: During peak lunch/dinner hours, delivery fees increase.
Railway Tatkal Tickets: Higher price for last-minute bookings (a form of surge pricing).

B. Auction Pricing

Price determined through bidding process. Highest bidder wins.

Example — Auction Pricing
eBay: Customers bid on products; highest bid at auction end wins.
Crypto Art (NFTs): Digital art sold through auctions on platforms like OpenSea.
Government Auctions: Spectrum auctions for telecom companies (Jio, Airtel, Vi bid for airwaves).
Used Cars (Cars24, Spinny): Reverse auction where dealers bid to buy seller's car.
Topic 7

Pricing in Online Marketing

Online marketing has introduced new pricing models that leverage digital capabilities like low distribution costs, customer data, and real-time adjustments. The most common online pricing models are Free, Premium, Freemium, and Token-based pricing.

1. Free Pricing

The product or service is offered completely free of charge. Revenue comes from other sources like advertising, cross-selling, or data monetization.

Example — Free Pricing in India
Google Search, Gmail, Google Maps: Free to users — Google earns from advertising.
Facebook, Instagram, YouTube, WhatsApp: Free to users — revenue from ads and business tools.
Hotstar (free tier): Free content supported by advertisements.
JioSaavn (free tier): Free music with ads.

2. Premium Pricing

Charging a high price for exclusive, high-quality, or feature-rich products. Signals superiority and exclusivity.

Example — Premium Pricing in Online Marketing
Apple Products (iPhone, MacBook): Premium pricing despite similar specifications to competitors.
Sony PlayStation 5: Premium pricing for gaming consoles.
Adobe Creative Cloud: ₹3,500+/month — premium pricing for professional software.

3. Freemium Pricing (Most Important for Online)

Freemium = Free + Premium. Basic version is free, advanced features are paid. This model attracts a large user base with free access, then converts a percentage to paying customers.

Zoho CRM: Free plan for up to 3 users. Paid plans start at ₹1,250/user/month. This allows small businesses to start free and upgrade as they grow. Many Indian SaaS companies (Zoho, Freshworks, Chargebee) successfully use freemium.

4. Token-Based Pricing

Token-based pricing is a model where customers purchase tokens (credits, coins, points) and redeem them for specific services or usage. Each service consumes a certain number of tokens.

Example — Token-Based Pricing
AI Services (OpenAI, Midjourney, Claude): You buy tokens/credits, each API call consumes tokens.
Cloud Services (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud): Pay per hour or per GB — token-based in essence.
Gaming (PUBG, Free Fire): Buy "UC" or "diamonds" to purchase in-game items (skins, weapons, emotes).
Online Stock Photo Sites (Shutterstock, Freepik): Buy credits, each image download costs credits.
UPI Apps (PhonePe, GPay): Scratch cards, coins, reward points — token-based loyalty programs.

Advantages of Token-Based Pricing

  • Customer pays only for what they use
  • Lower entry barrier (buy small token pack)
  • Predictable revenue for company (tokens expire)
  • Encourages customer lock-in (unused tokens)

Disadvantages

  • Customers may waste tokens
  • Complex pricing communication
  • Token expiry can create dissatisfaction
  • Requires tracking system
Online Pricing Summary

Online pricing models are flexible and customer-centric. Free builds user base, Premium maximizes margins, Freemium balances both, and Token-based monetizes usage. Successful online businesses often combine multiple models (e.g., Spotify: Free + Premium).

Topic 8

Price Change: Initiating & Responding to Price Changes

Companies often need to change prices — either increasing (to cover costs or improve margins) or decreasing (to gain market share or respond to competition). How companies initiate and respond to price changes is critical for success.

Part A: Initiating Price Cuts (Decreasing Price)

When Do Companies Cut Prices?

  • Excess Capacity: Too much production capacity, not enough demand.
  • Falling Market Share: Losing customers to competitors.
  • Cost Reduction: Lower costs allow lower prices (e.g., economies of scale).
  • Penetration Strategy: Enter new market with low prices.

Risks of Price Cuts (Price War)

  • Price War: Competitors also cut prices → everyone's profits shrink.
  • Lower Quality Perception: Customers may think cheaper = lower quality.
  • Shallow Pockets: Smaller competitors may go bankrupt.
Example — Price War in India (Telecom)
When Reliance Jio entered telecom with extremely low prices (free calls, cheap data), Airtel, Vodafone-Idea, and BSNL were forced to match prices. Result: Industry revenue dropped significantly. Several smaller players exited. Jio gained market share but at the cost of industry-wide profit erosion.

Part B: Initiating Price Increases (Increasing Price)

When Do Companies Increase Prices?

  • Cost Inflation: Raw materials, labor, energy costs increase.
  • Excess Demand: Demand exceeds supply capacity.
  • Brand Value: Premium brand can command higher prices.

How to Communicate Price Increases (Minimize Customer Anger)

  • Justify the Increase: Explain rising costs (transparency).
  • Grandfathering: Increase only for new customers, existing customers stay at old price for some time.
  • Reduce Product Size: Keep price same but reduce quantity (shrinkflation).
  • Add Value: Increase price but add new features or services.
Example — Price Increases in India
Milk (Amul, Mother Dairy): When cattle feed costs rise, companies announce "Inflation-adjusted price" and media covers it — customers understand.
Netflix India: Increased subscription prices, communicated via email with justification: "To continue bringing you great content." Some customers left, but loyal ones stayed.
Shrinkflation (Cadbury Dairy Milk): Instead of increasing ₹5 chocolate price, reduced weight from 15g to 12g. Customers notice less, but effective.

Part C: Responding to Competitors' Price Changes

When Competitor Cuts Price

  • Maintain Price & Add Value: Don't lower price, but add free service, extended warranty, better packaging.
  • Reduce Price Selectively: Only on specific products or in specific regions.
  • Introduce Economy Brand: Launch a lower-priced sub-brand to compete.
  • Do Nothing: If the price cut doesn't threaten your business, ignore.
  • Match Price: Only if you can still make profit at lower price.

When Competitor Increases Price

  • Do Nothing (Gain Customers): Customers may switch to you.
  • Also Increase Price: If you want to improve margins.
  • Increase Price & Add Value: Justify increase with improvements.
  • Launch Premium Product: Capture customers who are willing to pay more.
Example — Responding to Price Changes in India
Flipkart vs Amazon: During Big Billion Days vs Great Indian Festival, both match each other's prices almost instantly. They also respond with additional bank offers and free EMIs.
Swiggy vs Zomato: When one introduces "Zero delivery fee" promotion, the other responds within hours with similar or better offers.
Exam Tip

Remember: Price wars benefit CONSUMERS but harm INDUSTRY profits. Think of Jio vs Airtel — customers got cheaper data, but telecom companies' profits dropped.

Topic 9

Use of Big Data and Generative AI in Pricing Decisions

Big Data refers to large, complex datasets that can be analyzed to reveal patterns and insights. Generative AI refers to AI models (like ChatGPT) that can generate text, predictions, and recommendations. Together, they are revolutionizing pricing decisions by enabling real-time dynamic pricing, personalized pricing, and predictive analytics.

How Big Data Improves Pricing Decisions

ApplicationHow Big Data HelpsBusiness Example
Demand Forecasting Analyzing historical sales, seasonality, weather, holidays to predict future demand. Amazon adjusts prices before expected demand surge (e.g., higher umbrella prices before rain forecast).
Competitor Price Tracking Automated scraping of competitors' prices in real-time. Hotels.com tracks competitor rates and adjusts prices to stay competitive.
Customer Willingness to Pay Analyzing purchase history, browsing behavior, location, device to understand price sensitivity. E-commerce shows different prices to different customers based on their behavior (price discrimination).
Price Elasticity Calculation Running thousands of micro-experiments to measure how demand changes with price. Netflix tests different subscription prices in small markets before global changes.

How Generative AI (GenAI) Improves Pricing

📝

Price Recommendation Generation

GenAI models (like GPT-4) can analyze market data and recommend optimal prices with explanations.

Example: AI suggests "Increase price by 5% on premium product — demand elasticity is low"

💬

Price Communication

GenAI writes personalized emails and notifications explaining price changes to customers.

Example: "Dear customer, due to rising raw material costs, we have adjusted our prices by 3%"

⚡

Real-Time Dynamic Pricing

AI predicts competitor responses and customer reactions to price changes.

Example: Uber AI determines surge multiplier based on rider demand, driver supply, weather, events

Real-World Applications in India

CompanyHow Big Data/AI is Used for PricingExample
Ola / Uber Surge Pricing Algorithm: Real-time analysis of rider demand vs driver supply, traffic, weather, events. During heavy rain in Mumbai, surge multiplier increases from 1.0x to 2.5x automatically.
Amazon India Dynamic Repricing: Prices change multiple times per day based on competitor prices, demand, inventory. Amazon tracks Flipkart prices and auto-adjusts to stay competitive.
MakeMyTrip / Goibibo Hotel & Flight Pricing: Prices increase as booking date approaches and seats/rooms fill. Flight ticket ₹3000 when booked 30 days before; ₹8000 when booked 1 day before.
Swiggy / Zomato Delivery Fee & Surge: Peak hours, bad weather, low rider availability increase delivery fees. 8 PM dinner time delivery fee ₹30; 3 PM afternoon ₹10.
BigBasket / Blinkit Slot-Based Pricing: Dynamic pricing for delivery slots based on demand. Evening delivery slot ₹49, morning slot ₹19 (less demand).
Example — How Amazon Uses Big Data for Dynamic Pricing
Amazon's algorithm considers:
  • Competitor prices (scraped every few minutes)
  • Historical demand for the product
  • Time of day/day of week (weekends vs weekdays)
  • Customer browsing and purchase history
  • Inventory levels (low stock = price increase)
  • Seasonality (higher prices during festivals)
Result: A product's price can change multiple times per day — up to 2.5 million price changes daily on Amazon.

Benefits of AI/Big Data in Pricing

  • Real-time price optimization
  • Personalized pricing at scale
  • Better demand forecasting
  • Automated competitor response
  • Improved profit margins

Challenges & Risks

  • Algorithm collusion (unintentional price fixing)
  • Customer perception (unfair dynamic pricing)
  • Privacy concerns with personalized pricing
  • Regulatory scrutiny (CIPL, consumer courts)
  • Algorithm errors causing losses
Regulatory Note for India

The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 and DPDP Act, 2023 have implications for AI-driven personalized pricing. Unfair trade practices (e.g., charging different prices based on user data without transparency) may attract penalties. Companies must be transparent about dynamic pricing.

Future of Pricing

Big Data and Generative AI are transforming pricing from a static annual decision to a real-time, personalized, automated process. Companies that embrace AI-driven pricing gain competitive advantage, but must balance with ethics, transparency, and regulatory compliance.

Unit Summary

Key Takeaways — Pricing (Unit 02)

Important Points to Remember

• Price is the only element of the marketing mix that generates revenue — all other elements are costs.

• Setting price involves a 6-step process: Objectives → Demand → Costs → Competition → Method → Final Price.

• Internal factors affecting pricing: Costs, objectives, brand image, PLC stage.

• External factors: Competition, demand elasticity, economy, regulations, channel margins.

• Price adaptation includes: Geographical pricing, discounts, promotional pricing, differentiated pricing, transfer pricing, dynamic pricing (surge, auction).

• Online pricing models: Free, Premium, Freemium, Token-based.

• Price changes must be carefully managed — price wars destroy profits.

• Big Data & AI are enabling real-time dynamic pricing, personalized pricing, and predictive analytics.

Case Study 1 — Reliance Jio (Domestic - Price Change / Penetration Pricing)
Traditional Practice: Indian telecom had high prices (₹250+ for 1GB data). Airtel, Vodafone, Idea dominated market.
Contemporary Practice (Jio Entry 2016): Free voice calls for life, dirt-cheap data (₹50/GB), free services for first 6 months. Result: 100M+ customers in shortest time. Competitors forced to match prices, industry revenue fell, consolidation happened (Vodafone-Idea merger).
Key Learning: Aggressive penetration pricing can capture market share rapidly but triggers price wars that hurt industry profitability.
Case Study 2 — Ola/Uber Surge Pricing (Domestic - Dynamic Pricing)
Traditional Practice: Fixed metre-based taxi fares, same price 24/7 regardless of demand.
Contemporary Practice: Algorithmic surge pricing — prices increase when demand exceeds supply (peak hours, bad weather, festivals). Customer sees multiplier (e.g., 1.5x, 2x) before booking. Communication: "Surge pricing is active" — transparency helps acceptance.
Key Learning: Dynamic pricing manages demand-supply gaps but must be communicated transparently to avoid customer backlash.
Case Study 3 — Spotify Freemium (International - Online Pricing)
Traditional Practice: CDs sold at fixed price (₹500/album), radio was free with ads.
Contemporary Practice: Freemium model: Free tier (ads, limited skips, no offline) and Premium tier (₹119/month, ad-free, unlimited skips, offline, higher quality). Conversion rate: ~5% of free users convert to paid. Revenue from Premium + ad sales on free tier.
Key Learning: Freemium converts free users to paid by restricting desirable features (offline, no ads, unlimited skips). Free tier acts as acquisition channel.
Case Study 4 — Zoho Freemium & Token Pricing (Domestic - E-Product)
Product: Zoho CRM (Customer Relationship Management).
Contemporary Practice: Freemium — Free plan for up to 3 users. Paid plans: Standard (₹1,250/user/month), Professional (₹2,500/user/month), Enterprise (₹5,000/user/month). Also token-based pricing for API calls (pay per thousand API calls).
Key Learning: Freemium lowers entry barrier for startups. Upsell higher tiers as business grows. Token pricing monetizes usage beyond subscription.
