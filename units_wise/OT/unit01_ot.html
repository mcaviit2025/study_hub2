Part A

Decision Making Under UNCERTAINTY

Under uncertainty, the probabilities of states of nature are NOT known. Decision makers use different criteria based on their attitude toward risk.
Criterion 1

Maximax Criterion (Optimistic Approach)

Maximax is for the risk-taker / optimistic decision maker. The decision maker chooses the alternative that gives the maximum possible payoff. Formula: Maximax = max( max payoff of each row )
EXAMPLE 1.1 Simple Level

Real-world context:

A businessman has three alternative investment options (S₁, S₂, S₃) with four possible market conditions (N₁, N₂, N₃, N₄). Profits are in ₹ lakhs.

Payoff Matrix (Profit in ₹ lakhs):

N₁N₂N₃N₄
S₁8576
S₂4938
S₃10657
Step 1: Find the maximum payoff for each alternative (row maximum)
For S₁ = max(8, 5, 7, 6) = 8
For S₂ = max(4, 9, 3, 8) = 9
For S₃ = max(10, 6, 5, 7) = 10
Step 2: Find the maximum among these row maximums
Maximax = max(8, 9, 10) = 10
✅ Final Answer: Optimal strategy is S₃ with payoff ₹10 lakhs

Criterion 3

Laplace Criterion (Equal Probability Approach)

Laplace assumes all states of nature are equally likely. Probability = 1/n for n states. Choose the alternative with the highest average payoff. Formula: Average = (Sum of payoffs) / (Number of states)
EXAMPLE 3.1 Simple Level

Payoff Matrix (Profit in ₹ lakhs):

N₁N₂N₃
S₁504030
S₂454535
S₃405040
Step 1: Calculate average payoff for each alternative (3 states, so divide by 3)
S₁ = (50 + 40 + 30) / 3 = 120 / 3 = 40
S₂ = (45 + 45 + 35) / 3 = 125 / 3 = 41.67
S₃ = (40 + 50 + 40) / 3 = 130 / 3 = 43.33
Step 2: Find the maximum average payoff
Laplace = max(40, 41.67, 43.33) = 43.33
✅ Final Answer: Optimal strategy is S₃ with average payoff ₹43.33 lakhs
EXAMPLE 3.2 Medium Level

S₂ = (20 + 24 + 18 + 22) / 4 = 84 / 4 = 21
S₃ = (22 + 20 + 16 + 26) / 4 = 84 / 4 = 21
S₄ = (24 + 18 + 22 + 20) / 4 = 84 / 4 = 21

Criterion 4

Hurwicz Criterion (Realism Approach with α = 0.6)

Hurwicz balances optimism and pessimism using coefficient α (0 ≤ α ≤ 1). α = 0.6 means 60% weight to best outcome, 40% weight to worst outcome. Formula: Hurwicz Value = α(Max) + (1-α)(Min)
EXAMPLE 4.1 Simple Level

Payoff Matrix (Profit in ₹ lakhs):

N₁N₂N₃
S₁302015
S₂252520
S₃351812
Step 1: For each strategy, find Max and Min
S₁: Max = 30, Min = 15
S₂: Max = 25, Min = 20
S₃: Max = 35, Min = 12
Step 2: Apply formula: α(Max) + (1-α)(Min) with α = 0.6, (1-α) = 0.4
S₁ = 0.6 × 30 + 0.4 × 15 = 18 + 6 = 24
S₂ = 0.6 × 25 + 0.4 × 20 = 15 + 8 = 23
S₃ = 0.6 × 35 + 0.4 × 12 = 21 + 4.8 = 25.8
Step 3: Find the maximum value
Hurwicz = max(24, 23, 25.8) = 25.8
✅ Final Answer: Optimal strategy is S₃ with Hurwicz value ₹25.8 lakhs
EXAMPLE 4.2 Medium Level

S₂: Max = 35, Min = 30
S₃: Max = 38, Min = 28
S₄: Max = 40, Min = 25
Step 2: Apply formula with α=0.6, (1-α)=0.4
S₁ = 0.6×40 + 0.4×25 = 24 + 10 = 34
S₂ = 0.6×35 + 0.4×30 = 21 + 12 = 33
S₃ = 0.6×38 + 0.4×28 = 22.8 + 11.2 = 34
S₄ = 0.6×40 + 0.4×25 = 24 + 10 = 34
Step 3: Find the maximum
Hurwicz = max(34, 33, 34, 34) = 34
✅ Final Answer: Optimal strategies are S₁, S₃, or S₄ with Hurwicz value ₹34 crores

Criterion 5

Minimax Regret Criterion (Savage Criterion)

Savage Criterion (Minimax Regret) minimizes the maximum opportunity loss (regret). The decision maker wants to avoid the feeling of regret after making a decision.

📌 Steps to Solve Savage Criterion (Minimax Regret Criterion)

Step 1: Write the given payoff/cost matrix.
Step 2: Identify the best value in each state (column).
   • For Profit/Payoff Matrix → Find Maximum value of each column.
   • For Cost Matrix → Find Minimum value of each column.
Step 3: Construct the regret (opportunity loss) table.
   • For Profit Matrix: Regret = Column Maximum − Actual Payoff
   • For Cost Matrix: Regret = Actual Cost − Column Minimum
Step 4: Find the maximum regret for each strategy (row).
Step 5: Select the strategy having the minimum of maximum regrets.
Final Rule: The strategy with minimum maximum regret is the optimal strategy under Savage Criterion.
EXAMPLE 5.1 Simple Level (Profit Matrix)

Real-world context:

A businessman has three alternative investment strategies with three possible market conditions. Profits are in ₹ lakhs.

Step 1: Payoff Matrix (Profit in ₹ lakhs)

N₁N₂N₃
S₁604030
S₂555035
S₃506045
Step 2: Identify best value in each column (Maximum for Profit Matrix)
Column N₁: max(60, 55, 50) = 60
Column N₂: max(40, 50, 60) = 60
Column N₃: max(30, 35, 45) = 45
Step 3: Construct Regret Table (Regret = Column Maximum − Actual Payoff)
N₁N₂N₃
S₁60-60=060-40=2045-30=15
S₂60-55=560-50=1045-35=10
S₃60-50=1060-60=045-45=0
Step 4: Find maximum regret for each strategy (row)
S₁: max(0, 20, 15) = 20
S₂: max(5, 10, 10) = 10
S₃: max(10, 0, 0) = 10
Step 5: Select strategy with minimum of maximum regrets
min(20, 10, 10) = 10
Strategies S₂ and S₃ have the minimum maximum regret.
✅ Final Answer: Optimal strategies are S₂ or S₃ with maximum regret of ₹10 lakhs

Part B

Decision Making Under RISK

Under risk, the probabilities of states of nature are known. We use Expected Monetary Value (EMV), Expected Value with Perfect Information (EVwPI), and Value of Perfect Information (VPI/EVPI).
Risk Criterion 1

Expected Monetary Value (EMV)

EMV = Σ (Payoff × Probability) for each alternative. Choose the alternative with the highest EMV.
EXAMPLE 6.1 Simple Level (EMV)

Real-world context:

A businessman has three alternative actions (S₁, S₂, S₃) with three possible events (N₁, N₂, N₃) and known probabilities.

Payoff Table with Probabilities:

StatesN₁ (0.3)N₂ (0.6)N₃ (0.1)
S₁2018-9
S₂251510
S₃40-1012
Step 1: Calculate EMV for each strategy
EMV(S₁) = (20 × 0.3) + (18 × 0.6) + (-9 × 0.1)
= 6 + 10.8 + (-0.9) = 6 + 10.8 - 0.9 = 15.9
EMV(S₂) = (25 × 0.3) + (15 × 0.6) + (10 × 0.1)
= 7.5 + 9 + 1 = 17.5
EMV(S₃) = (40 × 0.3) + (-10 × 0.6) + (12 × 0.1)
= 12 + (-6) + 1.2 = 12 - 6 + 1.2 = 7.2
Step 2: Find maximum EMV
Maximum EMV = max(15.9, 17.5, 7.2) = 17.5
✅ Final Answer: Optimal strategy is S₂ with EMV ₹17.5 lakhs

C553532

EMV(A) = (50×0.2) + (40×0.5) + (30×0.3) = 10 + 20 + 9 = 39

Risk Criterion 2 & 3

EVwPI (Expected Value with Perfect Information) & EVPI (Value of Perfect Information)

EVwPI = Σ (Best payoff in each column × Probability)
EVPI = EVwPI − Maximum EMV
EVPI represents the maximum amount the decision maker would pay for perfect information.
EXAMPLE 7.1 Simple Level (EVwPI & EVPI)

Same payoff table as Example 6.1:

Step 1: Find the best payoff in each column (state)
For N₁: Best = max(20, 25, 40) = 40 (S₃)
For N₂: Best = max(18, 15, -10) = 18 (S₁)
For N₃: Best = max(-9, 10, 12) = 12 (S₃)
Step 2: Calculate EVwPI
EVwPI = (40 × 0.3) + (18 × 0.6) + (12 × 0.1)
= 12 + 10.8 + 1.2 = 24
Step 3: Calculate EVPI
From Example 6.1, Maximum EMV = 17.5
EVPI = EVwPI − Maximum EMV = 24 − 17.5 = 6.5
✅ Final Answer:
EVwPI = ₹24 lakhs
EVPI = ₹6.5 lakhs (maximum to pay for perfect information)

Unit Summary

Key Takeaways — Decision Theory

Important Points to Remember

Under UNCERTAINTY (Probabilities NOT known):
• Maximax: max(max of each row) — for optimistic decision makers
• Maximin: max(min of each row) — for pessimistic decision makers
• Laplace: max(average of each row) — assumes equal probability
• Hurwicz: α(Max) + (1-α)(Min) — balances optimism and pessimism
• Minimax Regret: min(max regret) — minimizes opportunity loss

Under RISK (Probabilities known):
• EMV: Σ(Payoff × Probability) — choose highest
• EVwPI: Σ(Best payoff × Probability)
• EVPI: EVwPI − Maximum EMV (value of perfect information)

Exam Tips
  • Always check if probabilities are given → Risk (EMV) else → Uncertainty
  • For Maximax & Maximin: Only need row operations
  • For Minimax Regret: First create regret table (column max − value)
  • For Hurwicz: α=0.6 means 60% weight to best outcome
  • EVPI is always a positive number representing maximum worth of perfect information
